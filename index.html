01 Our story

A table set by memory

A kitchen, a fire, and generations of memory.

Indian food was never meant to belong to one region, one recipe, or one table. It is a conversation between soil, season, spice and fire.

At Raasa, the kitchen moves through that conversation slowly. Vegetables meet the tandoor, lentils rest over charcoal, and marinades are given the time their ingredients ask for. We cook from the memory of family kitchens and the particularities of the places they came from.

“We don't recreate the past. We cook what it taught us.”
— The kitchen at Raasa

From the warm smoke of a tandoor to the bright lift of fresh coriander, every plate carries a little of where it began.

03   The heritage

The fire is part
of the recipe.

Before there was a timer, there was instinct. Before a kitchen had a name, there was the tandoor, the clay pot, the iron griddle, and a fire watched closely.

We use charcoal for its quiet depth, dum cooking for its patience, and hand-ground spices for their brightness. The techniques are old; the ingredients follow the season.
